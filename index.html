01 · The problem

Most neuromodulation is still open-loop.

A fixed dose is chosen once and delivered regardless of what the brain is doing. That ignores large differences between patients and from one session to the next, and leaves no objective record of why a dose was given.

Open-loop today

  • Same parameters every session, set by trial and error
  • Outcome judged by symptom scales weeks later
  • Deep targets need surgery (DBS, SCS); non-invasive tools reach mostly the cortex
  • Dose decisions are hard to reconstruct or audit

The StimLoop approach

  • A continuous neural feedback measure guides every parameter update
  • Per-patient calibration of response direction, gain and uncertainty
  • Non-invasive temporal interference to reach deep structures
  • Every request, delivery and decision is recorded, replayable and audited

02 · Platform

StimLoop: one control loop, any signal, any stimulator.

StimLoop is a general framework, not a single experiment. Signal sources, feedback measures, controllers and stimulators are plugins behind fixed interfaces. An experiment is a configuration pack that selects them. Safety supervision, recording, replay and audit stay the same whichever device is attached.

Stage 1 / 5

Signal input

    click a stage on the loop

    Two independent gates

    HOLD / STEP decides whether parameters may change. ALLOW / WAIT / SUSPEND decides whether output may occur. The stricter gate always wins.

    Delivery needs evidence

    A request or an acknowledgement does not prove delivery. Unknown output is never retried automatically, and unresolved reservations stay charged to the session budget.

    No pain classifier required

    Control runs on a calibrated, continuous neural measure. Disease labels stay out of the core, so one framework serves many indications.

    Reproducible by design

    JSONL/HDF5 records, hash-bound manifests, checkpoints and shadow-decision replay. An independent auditor rebuilds each decision from the records alone.

    04 · In-house hardware

    tTIS: deep brain stimulation without surgery.

    Temporal interference uses two kHz-range currents at slightly different frequencies. Each carrier alone is too fast to drive neurons, but where they overlap they produce a slow envelope at the difference frequency Δf. That envelope can be placed below the cortex by changing electrode positions and the current ratio. Our stimulator is designed from the ground up for this method and for closed-loop control.

    Interference, live

    Envelope10 Hz
    Channel 22010 Hz
    Modulation depth100%

    Steer the focus

    Illustrative 2-D homogeneous conductor, not a head model. Shifting the current ratio moves the envelope peak between the electrode pairs without moving any electrode. A single pair, by contrast, is strongest at the scalp.

    05 · Neural state models

    From raw voltage to a number a controller can trust.

    A closed loop is only as good as its feedback. We build state models across three recording scales: scalp EEG for the clinic, ECoG for translational studies, and in vivo arrays for mechanism. Each model is quality-controlled, causal where it feeds control, and analysed with the animal or subject as the statistical unit.

    State monitor illustrative

    in target

    EEG · non-invasive

    Clinical readout

    Band power, spectral shape and event detection, with artifact-aware QC that keeps EMG, EOG and ECG channels in their own roles. This is the feedback channel for a non-invasive therapy.

    ECoG · translational

    Anesthesia depth & emergence

    A blinded 16-channel ECoG pipeline: burst-suppression ratio, spectral and connectivity features, phase-amplitude coupling, CSD and topomaps. Recovery composites are tested with permutation statistics. It is already used to study tTIS-modulated recovery.

    In vivo · mechanism

    LFP & spiking activity

    Grids of up to 64 channels for LFP and MUA, spike sorting through SpikeInterface, and event-aligned responses that link a stimulation parameter to circuit-level effects.

    06 · Lead program
